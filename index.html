TermsGuard AI

Paste a privacy policy or terms of service. It reads the clauses, flags the ones that work against you, and ties each flag to the GDPR article behind it. Every flag is checked by a model before you see it, and the source text it came from is shown.

What leaves your machine. The text you paste is compared against a local reference index of 4,527 chunks. Retrieval, embeddings and the index all run on this server. Up to 15 flagged sentences are sent to Anthropic for verification, nothing else is, the connection is encrypted in transit, and no text is written to disk.

How it works

  1. Split. The text is broken into individual clauses and duplicates are removed.
  2. Match. Each clause is embedded and compared by cosine similarity against 48 labelled fair clauses and 48 labelled unfair ones.
  3. Ground. A matching passage is retrieved from a 4,527 chunk reference index for each candidate, so you can read the context the flag was drawn from.
  4. Verify. Claude Haiku 4.5 rules on every confident candidate, in both directions. It can dismiss a false positive and it can overturn a clause that was wrongly called protective.

The summary is assembled by template from the clauses that were matched and verified. No model writes it, so a legal citation cannot be invented. The article shown on each flag is the one the model named. The retrieved passage beside it is context, not the article text.

Educational project. Not legal advice.

The analysis is produced by a language model and can be wrong. It is a way of finding the clauses worth reading, not a substitute for a lawyer. Check the source text before you rely on anything here.
