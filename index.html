Paste any privacy policy or terms of service. It reads every clause, flags the ones that work against you, and shows you the GDPR article it matched, and the passage the flag was drawn from. Every flag is ruled on by a verification model before it lands, in both directions, so a wrongly-flagged protection is overturned as visibly as a missed violation.

How it works

  1. Split. The text is broken into individual clauses and duplicates are removed.
  2. Match. Each clause is embedded and compared by cosine similarity against 52 hand-labelled fair clauses and 50 hand-labelled unfair ones.
  3. Ground. A matching passage is retrieved from a 550-chunk reference index of the actual GDPR text, so the context beside each flag is the law itself, not a related lecture note.
  4. Verify. When a verification model is configured, it rules on every confident candidate, in both directions. It can dismiss a false positive and it can overturn a clause that was wrongly called protective.
Why no fabricated citations. The summary is assembled by template from the clauses that were matched and verified, so a legal citation cannot be invented. The article shown on each flag is the one the matching reference clause was labelled with. The retrieved passage beside it is the law text, not a related document.

Educational project. Findings are produced by a language model and can be wrong. They are a way of finding the clauses worth reading, not a substitute for a lawyer. Check the source text before relying on anything here.
